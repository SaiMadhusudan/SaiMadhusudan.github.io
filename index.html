Sai Madhusudan Gunda

M.S. by Research, Computer Science, IIIT Hyderabad  ·  Research Engineer, BharatGen

My research lies at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, with a focus on document intelligence: teaching models to read, ground, and reason over complex documents, from modern PDFs to centuries-old palm-leaf manuscripts written in low-resource Indic scripts.

I am broadly interested in how vision-language models can move beyond pattern matching toward grounded reasoning, where every prediction traces back to specific visual evidence. This thread runs through much of my work: M3Grounder (CVPR 2026) introduces mask-based multi-granular grounding at phrase, line, and block levels; DoCoG (ECCV 2026 Oral) extends this to grounded chain-of-thought reasoning over both textual and graphical elements; and DocLayout-VL (ECCV 2026) proposes a promptable foundational model for hierarchical document layout.

In parallel, I work on OCR for historical Indic manuscripts, where script diversity, physical degradation, and data scarcity collide. UniLipi (ICDAR 2026) unifies OCR across 13 Indic scripts with script-aware synthetic data, and CURIO (WACV 2026) addresses curvature and warping in degraded manuscripts. I am currently developing Patram-7B-Instruct at BharatGen, India's National AI Mission. Patram is a multilingual vision-language model for document understanding, trained across 256 H100 GPUs.

More broadly, I want to keep asking how models can see, understand, and reason about complex documents in a way that stays faithful to the evidence on the page, especially for the languages, scripts, and archives that today's foundation models still leave behind.

I am advised by Prof. Ravi Kiran Sarvadevabhatla at CVIT.
